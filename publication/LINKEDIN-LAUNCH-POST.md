I evaluated Reverse Engineer Anything across seven real-world applications to understand what it adds when an AI agent investigates unfamiliar software.

The hands-on work covered SAP AI workflows, native barcode generation, desktop search, an Android calendar, browser drawing and image compression. REA supplied useful structured observations, artifact identities and retained evidence. Across these studies, I did not establish an incremental functional reconstruction advantage over the corresponding controls.

The details were revealing: all three calendar reconstructions missed a one-second reminder adjustment, one comparison was invalid, and an image-decoding result exposed a grading false negative. My article explains the findings and their limits. I welcome discussion about how engineers should test functional understanding alongside reverse-engineering evidence.
